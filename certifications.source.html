ISI, FMCS and CRS: how each certification works

Which scheme you need depends on the product and where it is made. Here is the procedure, an indicative timeline and the documents for each.

ISI Mark (mandatory and voluntary)FMCSCRSProducts covered

ISI Mark (BIS Scheme-I)

For factories in India. BIS audits your factory, tests samples and grants a licence to mark products with the ISI Mark.

Mandatory

The product is covered by a QCO. It cannot be made, imported or sold in India without the mark.

Voluntary

An Indian Standard exists but no QCO applies. Optional, and useful for tenders, retail listings and buyer trust. The process is the same.

Procedure

  1. Identify the Indian Standard and check whether a QCO covers your product.
  2. Prepare factory documents and your quality control plan (Scheme of Testing and Inspection).
  3. File the application on the BIS portal and pay the fee.
  4. BIS scrutinises the application.
  5. A BIS officer inspects the factory and draws samples for testing at a recognised laboratory.
  6. BIS grants the licence. You mark products with the ISI Mark and licence number, and BIS monitors through surveillance.

Documents required

  • Company registration (incorporation, GST, Udyam)
  • Proof of factory premises (ownership or lease)
  • Factory layout and manufacturing process flowchart
  • Machinery and test equipment list with calibration records
  • Quality control plan and acceptance of the Scheme of Testing and Inspection
  • Organisation chart and authorised signatory letter
  • Raw material and critical component details
  • Test reports, where required
  • Trademark or brand details for marking, and fee payment proof

FMCS (Foreign Manufacturers Certification Scheme)

Scheme-I for factories outside India. You get the same ISI Mark, with one extra requirement: an Authorised Indian Representative (AIR).

Procedure

  1. Appoint an AIR who lives in India and handles all communication with BIS.
  2. Identify the Indian Standard and collect factory and product data.
  3. Prepare documents, signed and stamped by the manufacturer.
  4. The AIR files the application with BIS. Hard copies are still required.
  5. BIS scrutinises the documents.
  6. BIS officers inspect your overseas factory, and samples are tested.
  7. BIS grants the licence. You accept the Scheme of Testing and Inspection and pay the annual marking fee.
  8. Surveillance and renewal follow.

Indicative timeline

Document preparation
Depends on your factory records and translations.
Inspection scheduling
Commonly quoted at 10 to 20 days once scrutiny is complete.
Licence term
Usually one year initially, with extension possible.

Overall time varies with BIS and laboratory workload. We confirm an estimate after document review.

Documents required

  • Factory or company registration, with English translation
  • Manufacturing process flowchart, from raw material to packing
  • Factory layout plan
  • Machinery and test equipment list
  • Quality control manual and records
  • AIR appointment letter and AIR details
  • Acceptance of the Scheme of Testing and Inspection and annual marking fee
  • Test reports, and details of any outsourced processes (these need BIS approval)
  • Signed and stamped hard-copy sets of the application

CRS (Compulsory Registration Scheme)

For notified electronics and IT products. Registration is based on laboratory testing and is granted to the factory, not to an office.

Procedure

  1. Identify your product category and standard, for example IS 13252 (Part 1).
  2. Register on the BIS CRS portal. Foreign manufacturers appoint an AIR.
  3. Raise a test request and send samples to a BIS-recognised laboratory.
  4. The laboratory issues the test report.
  5. Apply online within 90 days of the report, with all supporting documents.
  6. BIS scrutinises the application and may ask for clarifications.
  7. BIS grants the registration number. You mark the product with the CRS logo and number.
  8. Random surveillance and renewal follow.

Indicative timeline

Laboratory testing
Depends on the product and laboratory slot.
Test report validity
90 days. Apply within this window.
BIS approval
Around 15 days once the application is complete.
Overall
Often 4 to 6 weeks, longer if laboratory slots are full.
Registration term
2 years, renewable.

Documents required

  • Test report from a BIS-recognised laboratory (not older than 90 days)
  • Authorisation letter for the applicant and the brand owner, where different
  • Declaration and undertaking, signed and notarised where required
  • Critical components list with their test reports or certificates
  • Model list, circuit diagram or bill of materials
  • Product photographs, label artwork and user manual
  • Company registration (incorporation, GST, Udyam)
  • AIR documents, for foreign manufacturers

Company name, model numbers and descriptions must match across the application, test report and labels.
